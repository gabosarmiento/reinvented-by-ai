# Changelog

This file records intellectual and structural changes during development of the book. It is not a sentence-level change log and does not form part of the reader-facing narrative.

## Unreleased

### Chapters 14–18 — Scaling, management, and accountability

- Reframed Fractal Unit as a proposed operating lens; added recent, bounded team-boundary evidence and recorded the required comparison with existing team/platform models.
- Added current OECD employer-survey evidence on algorithmic management concerns to Chapter 15, without claiming that AI matching or portfolio ranking improves outcomes.
- Updated Chapter 16 with 2025 OWASP agentic risk taxonomy and 2026 NIST agent identity work; clarified that guidance and protocol safeguards do not prove control effectiveness.
- Updated Chapter 17 with recent randomized evidence on work patterns and team collaboration; removed unsupported headcount thresholds and bounded organizational recommendations as hypotheses.
- Updated Chapter 18 against current EU AI Act text and Commission timelines; separated statutory oversight from operational accountability and left qualified legal review open.
- Added source-ledger entries and chapter-specific audit, research, argument, evidence, style, and review notes. Final production checks remain open.

### Chapter 1 — When Production Gets Cheap

- Refreshed the evidence to 28 September 2026: replaced a productivity study based on 2020–2021 data with 2026 randomized developer field experiments and a clearly bounded 2025 METR comparison.
- Added Stanford’s latest organizational-adoption figures, separating AI adoption, generative AI use, and agent deployment.
- Added a September 2026 preprint on token prices versus estimated cost per completed task, labeled preliminary and statistically unresolved.
- Tightened several stock contrast sentences and updated the evidence ledger and quality report.

### Chapter 2 — Hidden Operational Debt

- Labeled the six-part taxonomy as an author-developed diagnostic lens, given the limited empirical literature on organizational debt.
- Added a debt register that separates the unresolved design gap from recurring work or exposure and avoids unsupported monetary precision.
- Qualified AI-related pressure points as hypotheses to test; labeled vendor materials as product guidance rather than impact evidence.
- Replaced invented operational quantities with qualitative, explicitly illustrative examples.

### Chapter 3 — Why Tools Do Not Change the Operating Model

- Updated the Microsoft field-study account to its November 2025 revision, distinguishing frequent-user and intent-to-treat email estimates and specifying sample and scope.
- Bounded the inference: individual access to one office assistant did not measurably change task composition or meeting time in this study; that is not a universal law about AI adoption.
- Added chapter-specific audit, research, argument, and review notes and corrected the progress tracker to leave final production checks open.

### Chapter 4 — ATOM: Intent In, Evidence Out

- Framed the six-layer architecture and control plane consistently as this book's proposed model, not a validated or complete industry standard.
- Removed a duplicated Playbook definition and clarified owner approval for changes to production behavior.
- Labeled the refund walk-through illustrative and bounded claims that the model transfers to other decisions.

### Chapter 5 — Context Is Infrastructure

- Reframed the context envelope as a proposed tool whose boundaries must be validated against real cases.
- Separated Anthropic engineering advice and NIST voluntary risk guidance from independent evidence; clarified that source labels and prompts are not security boundaries.
- Updated prompt-injection discussion to stress limited impact and externally enforced authority, and added access/retention guidance for sample data.

### Chapters 6–13 — Decisions, execution, governance, and first loop

- Calibrated decision-rights and human-review guidance as proposals; added current NIST automation-bias context and explicit limits of first-party security advice.
- Updated the multi-agent discussion to include company-reported internal evaluation scope and token-cost multipliers; kept organization-level outcomes unclaimed.
- Corrected the fictional week scenario's blanket current-tool feasibility statement and retained its illustrative label.
- Updated MCP authorization and EU AI Act schedule language against current July/August 2026 sources.
- Clarified that dashboard metrics are not outcome evidence and that the pilot/build/value gates are decision aids, not validated formulas or promised timelines.
- Added chapter-specific audit, evidence, argument, and editorial review notes; left final production status open.

- Replaced the generic product-team opening with a labeled founder-COO composite: a company moving from about 40 people toward 150, adding a business line, and trying to move the COO from the run to organization design.
- Made the gap between delegated execution and retained decision authority the chapter’s leadership problem.
- Added Joshua Gans’s microeconomic distinction between cheaper prediction and the complete decision, then separated that source-backed frame from ATOM’s extension to tool-using agents, bounded authority, evidence, and recovery.
- Replaced invented “AI time” ratios and inevitability rhetoric with evidence on fixed-capability inference prices, bounded agent tasks, adoption, and heterogeneous productivity.
- Changed the opening argument from “AI moves faster than legacy companies” to “cheap production moves the operating constraint.”
- Introduced the distinction among output, throughput, outcome, and value.
- Added the five-queue diagnostic: selection, context, integration, verification, and authorization, with accountability surrounding the flow.
- Added explicit boundary conditions and a counterargument about agents absorbing downstream queues.
- Added a five-step Monday decision-delegation test and connected unresolved queues to Hidden Operational Debt.
- Deferred the definition of ATOM until Chapter 4 so the model is earned rather than announced.
- Replaced generic AI-wave illustrations with a version-controlled Mermaid diagram.

# Chapter 3 argument map

## Proposition

Local AI adoption may improve an individual task while leaving shared work arrangements unchanged; managers should measure the whole path and name the level of change accurately.

## Support

1. A recent randomized field experiment found individual work-pattern effects without detectable task-composition or meeting-time changes from individual access.
2. The chapter distinguishes task, workflow, and operating-model changes by the arrangements affected.
3. The discount and onboarding examples show how automation can retain decision rights and data dependencies.
4. End-to-end measures and counter-metrics can expose cost transfer to downstream teams.

## Objection and response

**Objection:** A local productivity gain can still be valuable and might later fund wider change.

**Response:** Agreed. The chapter says local improvements are legitimate; the concern is overstating their scope or value before measuring downstream work and outcomes.

## Boundary

The research is one office-assistant experiment, not proof of a universal adoption sequence. Task/workflow/operating-model is the book's practical classification, not a validated causal law.

## Decision

For a material pilot, name the level of change, identify the complete path to outcome, assign an end-to-end owner, and track at least one counter-metric.

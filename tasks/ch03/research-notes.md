# Chapter 3 research notes

Checked: 29 September 2026

## Primary empirical source

**Dillon, Jaffe, Immorlica, and Stanton (2025), “Shifting Work Patterns with Generative AI.”** The November 2025 NBER revision reports a six-month randomized field experiment across 66 firms and 7,137 knowledge workers. Among treated workers using the tool in more than half of sampled weeks, weekly email time declined by 3.6 hours; the intent-to-treat estimate was 1.3 hours. The study reports no significant change in meeting time and no shift in task quantity or composition from individual access. This is one integrated office assistant, not a test of every AI tool or a redesigned team workflow. The authors' Microsoft affiliation and study context should be visible in notes.

Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w33795

## Evidence boundary

All figures and the underlying experiment are from 2025, within 24 months of this review. The chapter's process examples are illustrative. The experiment supports the bounded observation that individual tool access can affect individual routines without measurable changes to coordinated work; it does not establish that task, workflow, and operating-model outcomes always separate in this way.

# Chapter 3 audit notes

Reviewed: 29 September 2026

## Existing chapter argument

The chapter distinguishes task-level productivity, workflow redesign, and changes to the operating model. It illustrates how automation can reduce local effort while retaining unclear decision rights, data gaps, and handoffs. Its intended reader is a founder or COO assessing whether an AI pilot changed how the company operates.

## Reuse and editorial direction

- Retain the useful definition of an operating model, the discount example, the onboarding example, and the task/workflow/operating-model table.
- Update the field experiment to the latest 2025 revision and report sample, outcome, and limits precisely.
- Change universal statements about where adoption or measured gains appear into conditional claims.
- Treat examples as illustrative; do not imply the hypothetical process is observed company data.
- Keep the adoption threshold useful, but qualify it as a proposed management practice rather than a validated universal rule.
- Ensure the pilot audit prompt asks for evidence and alternative explanations without implying an AI can independently verify business impact.

## Open issues

- The outline calls for a real randomized field-experiment case; the cited Microsoft study meets that requirement as external research, not author experience.
- No author operating-transformation case is required by this chapter.
- Final production and multi-format checks remain open.

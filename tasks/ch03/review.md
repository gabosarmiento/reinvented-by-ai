# Chapter 3 editorial review

Reviewed: 29 September 2026

## Findings and dispositions

### High — the field-study description was stale and overinterpreted

The text described an early, smaller version and presented the authors' interpretation as a general explanation. Updated it to the November 2025 NBER revision (66 firms, 7,137 workers), separated the frequent-user estimate from intent-to-treat, and stated the scope limits. The chapter's conclusion is now explicitly bounded to individual access in this study.

### Medium — some claims about pilots and coordination read as universal

Reworded the adoption inference as a management risk, not a guaranteed outcome. The task/workflow/operating-model distinction is identified in research notes as a practical classification, not a validated causal law.

### Medium — the chapter could undervalue local productivity

The text already says a task-level gain can be worth the license and can fund redesign. Kept that qualification and clarified that the issue is inaccurate scope/value claims.

### Medium — AI audit prompt could imply independent verification

Added explicit requirements for source evidence and strongest alternative explanation. The operator remains responsible for checking the assistant's analysis against records.

## COO test

- Local gains remain legitimate; the chapter does not demand an enterprise transformation for every tool.
- The examples expose how a tool can leave decision rights, authoritative data, and exception ownership untouched.
- The end-to-end owner and counter-metric offer a practical way to look for cost transfer.
- “Operating-model change” requires a named mechanism and changed behavior, not merely adoption or output volume.

## Remaining release conditions

Final production checks (terminology, links, cases, references, and HTML/EPUB/PDF rendering) have not been run. No claims are made about current rendering or publishing readiness.

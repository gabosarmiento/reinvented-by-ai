# Chapter 6 argument map

## Proposition

Before assigning work to an agent, define the recurring commitment, its owner, permitted authority, evidence, and escalation path.

## Support

1. A decision includes trigger, state, constraints, action, and consequences—not only a predicted answer.
2. Consequence, reversibility, frequency, detectability, and affected parties help shape a risk posture.
3. Access, recommendation, execution, and modification are separate permissions.
4. Human review requires usable evidence and actual authority; a nominal approval is not sufficient by itself.
5. Escalation patterns can expose missing context, overly narrow authority, or a poor policy.

## Objection and response

**Objection:** Decision inventories and envelopes may slow teams or put every choice through central governance.

**Response:** Inventory recurring commitments, delegate within explicit local bounds, and reserve central review for cross-unit or high-consequence choices. Match evidence and controls to consequence.

## Boundary

The matrix is a proposed starting tool, not a validated autonomy algorithm. Human approval can fail; risk is task- and design-dependent.

## Decision

Choose a recurring decision, specify its owner and limits, replay hard cases, and measure outcomes and review cost before expanding delegation.

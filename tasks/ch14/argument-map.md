# Chapter 14 argument map

## Claim

As a company scales across offers and units, clear outcome ownership, decision rights, and shared-service interfaces may help locate coordination costs. This chapter proposes a repeatable unit contract as one design lens.

## Support

- Team boundary management is associated with team performance in recent synthesis, but effects are contingent (S032).
- Unit contract, interface, and lifecycle details are author-proposed practical instruments, not validated predictors.
- The fictional Aster case carries the same pricing and usage assumptions from Chapter 2 through 100, 800, and 4,000 customers. It illustrates where unit ownership and shared platform interfaces might become useful; its numbers are not evidence or a forecast.

## Limits

- Overlaps with established team and platform models; substantive comparison still required.
- No causal evidence that this model improves performance or reduces coordination cost.
- Re-teaming costs and sector-specific constraints need context-specific evaluation.
- The proposed enterprise, growth, regulated-industry, and regional boundaries are options to test, not a prescribed structure.

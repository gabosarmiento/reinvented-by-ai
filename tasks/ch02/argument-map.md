# Chapter 2 Argument Map

Chapter: Hidden Operational Debt  
Refreshed: 28 September 2026

## Core proposition

As organizations produce more work, unresolved design gaps can reappear as waiting, handoffs, rework, exceptions, and concentrated decision load. Those costs become visible when workflow traces are connected to outcomes and reviewed by an accountable owner.

## Primary argument

1. **Premise:** Performance dashboards report outcomes but often omit the recurring work needed to produce them.
2. **Evidence:** Current operational systems hold timestamps, escalations, overrides, reopens, and customer or economic results; recent agent evaluation practices also distinguish traces from end states.
3. **Inference:** Repeated divergence between the intended path and actual work is an operating liability. AI may increase the number of cases reaching a weak boundary, but the effect outside software must be tested locally.
4. **Recommendation:** Map one workflow, classify its evidence with provisional lenses, connect the path to an outcome, and keep a register with an owner and a test.

## Supporting claims

- Six overlapping categories help search for decision, context, coordination, integration, knowledge, and control debt.
- Principal and interest are useful metaphors only when tied to observable gaps and recurring effects; they are not automatic monetary valuations.
- An AI-generated pattern is a hypothesis until operators verify it against source records.

## Skeptical objection and response

**Objection:** This is ordinary process waste with a new label.  
**Response:** The term earns its place only when it identifies a recurring liability that compounds across decisions and constrains scale. The register makes the claim testable; if the evidence shows a one-time defect, fix the defect without invoking debt.

## Boundary and falsification

The six-category model is an author-developed diagnostic lens, not validated theory. It is weakened if categories do not help operators locate recurring cost, predict where work will stall, or select an effective intervention. Data gaps and selection effects must be recorded.

## Monday decision

Choose one recurring workflow that returns to the COO. Trace its last several months of cases, connect wait and rework to customer or economic outcomes, and test one bounded change with a named owner.

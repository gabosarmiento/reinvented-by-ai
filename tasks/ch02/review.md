# Chapter 2 Editorial Review

Reviewed: 28 September 2026

## High findings

### H1 — The six categories could be mistaken for established research

**Finding:** The manuscript introduced a taxonomy without a research base validating its categories.  
**Resolution:** Added the 2024 literature context and called the categories an author-developed diagnostic lens. Their usefulness is framed as testable rather than established.

### H2 — AI's effect on debt was asserted too generally

**Finding:** The original language implied a cross-company effect from software-delivery evidence.  
**Resolution:** Limited DORA to software delivery, marked wider application as a hypothesis, and changed the table to possible pressures to test.

### H3 — The debt metaphor risked false precision

**Finding:** Principal and interest could imply a monetary model.  
**Resolution:** Defined them as operating-design gap and recurring work/exposure. Recommended natural units until a monetary conversion is defensible.

## Medium findings

### M1 — The chapter required an author-experience case

**Finding:** The approved outline calls for a transformation case from the author's experience, but none is present.  
**Disposition:** Outstanding author input. The Aster scenario is explicitly fictional; no personal or client case was invented.

### M2 — Product sources could sound like independent evidence

**Resolution:** Labeled Anthropic and LangChain material as first-party guidance/documentation and Intercom's metric revision as vendor documentation. No business-impact claim is attributed to them.

### M3 — The operational-debt measurement path needed a usable artifact

**Resolution:** Added a register with evidence, principal, recurring interest signal, owner, test, and review/expiry.

## Skeptical-COO test

- “Is this just process waste with a new name?” The chapter now says the term earns its place only if it identifies recurring liabilities that constrain scale, and the register permits local testing.
- “Will this turn into surveillance?” The chapter defines the workflow as the default unit and bars individual leaderboards.
- “Can a model decide which problems are real?” The owner reviews source records and validates each pattern before it becomes a metric or intervention.
- “Can we turn every cost into euros?” No; natural units are retained until a defensible conversion exists.

## Remaining release condition

The chapter is editorially revised, but finalization remains open until the author supplies or explicitly waives the author-experience case and the chapter passes the book's final production gate.

## Aster illustration

Replaced the generic business-line scenario with a fictional infrastructure company whose four-stage revenue and workload arithmetic uses one stated pricing equation. Implementation fees are excluded from ARR. Fleet authority-unit and operation volumes are products of the chosen assumptions, not market evidence. The case links growth to observable onboarding, review, support, incident, reconciliation, and authority-change work without assigning universal thresholds. It does not satisfy the author-experience release condition above.

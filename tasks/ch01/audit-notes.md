# Chapter 1 Audit Notes

Chapter: When Production Gets Cheap  
Audit completed: 11 September 2026

## Sources inspected

- `ebook v2 en.pdf`, pages 1–3.
- `ebook v4 en.pdf`, pages 4–7, including layout and illustrations.
- Related premises repeated in v4 Chapter 2 were noted but reserved for Chapter 2.

## Original chapter function

The source opening tries to create urgency through a fictional revelation, compresses time through invented ratios, declares legacy models obsolete, and introduces ATOM as a necessary replacement. It moves directly from “AI is fast” to “the organization must be rebuilt” without demonstrating the causal link.

v2 is more overtly generated and categorical. v4 adds organizational friction and agents but preserves the same sales structure. Neither version distinguishes task production from system value.

## Disposition

### KEEP

- Scaling is no longer synonymous with adding headcount or buying software.
- Faster AI-mediated production can expose slow planning and approval cycles.
- The relevant design object is the organization, not only the AI tool.
- There is a gap between the speed at which a system can generate work and the speed at which an organization can use it.

### EVOLVE

- Replace “AI clock speed” with a moving-constraint argument.
- Replace “legacy models” as one undifferentiated enemy with specific bottlenecks: selection, context, integration, verification, authorization, and accountability.
- Replace inevitability with heterogeneous evidence and boundary conditions.
- Reframe smaller-team leverage as a hypothesis about production capacity, not a promise of headcount reduction or value.

### MERGE

- Move operational-friction examples into Chapter 2’s Hidden Operational Debt taxonomy.
- Reserve tool-versus-system arguments for Chapter 3.
- Reserve the definition of ATOM for Chapter 4. Chapter 1 may establish the design problem but will not announce the branded answer.

### REMOVE

- Fictional executive revelation and boardroom mind-reading.
- “The wave is coming/here.”
- “One year equals a decade” and “15 AI days equals five traditional months.”
- “Adapt now or be left behind,” “operating on borrowed time,” and “survival demands.”
- “AI is oxygen.”
- Claims that all Agile-at-scale models are obsolete.
- Claims that ATOM is a necessary rupture before the model is explained or tested.
- Movie-rental illustration and generic AI-wave art.

### VERIFY

- AI inference cost decline for fixed capability.
- Expansion of agent task capability and the limits of benchmark interpretation.
- Productivity effects across customer support and experienced software development.
- Organizational adoption versus production agent deployment.
- Evidence that local production gains are absorbed or amplified by system conditions.

### NEW MATERIAL NEEDED

- Definitions of production, throughput, outcome, and value.
- A five-queue moving-constraint model: selection, context, integration, verification, authorization.
- The maintenance and option costs created by cheap generation.
- The difference between a local productivity result and an organizational operating result.
- A clear transition to Hidden Operational Debt.

## Distinctive author idea recovered

The original phrase “scaling means fundamentally rebuilding your organization’s DNA” is rhetorically inflated but contains the key author insight: a large enough change in the cost and agency of productive work changes organizational design. Preserve that causal direction, not the biological metaphor.

## Reuse rationale

No source sentence is strong enough to require verbatim reuse. The book preserves the author’s problem selection and rebuilds the argument from evidence. The opening will be quieter, more specific, and harder to dismiss: some production is becoming cheap; value is not; therefore the operating constraint moves.

## Author-review revision, 12 September 2026

The first draft aimed at a generic product team and an established enterprise operator. That audience was too broad. Chapter 1 now opens with an **ILLUSTRATIVE COMPOSITE** of a founder-COO scaling from roughly 40 people toward 150 while adding a business line and handing the run to a Head of Operations.

The public manuscript will not mention the abandoned source drafts or imply an earlier public edition. Decision 0006 records the distinction between internal provenance and reader-facing narrative.

# Chapter 4 argument map

## Proposition

An operating loop needs explicit intent, authorized context, decisions, execution, evidence, learning, and controls; ATOM is this book's proposed way to draw and inspect those elements together.

## Support

1. Six named layers make the flow inspectable.
2. A cross-cutting control plane makes authority and intervention visible.
3. The refund example shows how an incomplete action can be distinguished from a completed outcome.
4. Explicit boundaries keep ATOM from being mistaken for an autonomous company or technology stack.

## Objection and response

**Objection:** This may be a relabeling of familiar operating and governance practices.

**Response:** The chapter does not claim invention or validation. It offers a synthesis and drawing convention; the reader should use it only where it improves a specific decision loop.

## Boundary

The chapter establishes the model's vocabulary, not evidence that adopting ATOM improves performance. That requires tested applications, comparison with alternatives, and outcome data.

## Decision

Use the diagram to expose a missing owner, authority, evidence, or recovery path. Do not adopt it as a new bureaucracy or claim outcomes from drawing it.

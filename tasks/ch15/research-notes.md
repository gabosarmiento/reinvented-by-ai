# Chapter 15 research notes

Evidence checked 29 September 2026. The chapter's allocation practices are proposals; the current sources do not establish that they improve enterprise returns.

## Workforce allocation and algorithmic management

- Milanez, Lemmens, and Ruggiu (OECD, 6 February 2025) report employer survey findings about algorithmic management, including manager-perceived decision-quality changes and concerns around bias, explainability, accountability, worker health, and notice. Added as S033 and cited with scope limits.
- Limitation: employer/manager report, not worker outcome measurement and not a study of internal talent marketplaces or AI matching.
- The narrative assignment scenario is illustrative. Its six-week duration and participants are fictional, not measured data.

## Dynamic funding and knowledge

- Staged funding, cadence matched to reversibility, and opportunity-cost visibility are recommendations in this chapter, not results from a current controlled study.
- Knowledge products and AI-assisted allocation scenarios are proposed operating practices. Avoid claims of demonstrated business impact.

## Open research needs

- Independent evidence on internal talent marketplaces, worker consent, bias, privacy, and distribution of opportunity.
- Recent causal evidence on dynamic portfolio allocation in operating companies.

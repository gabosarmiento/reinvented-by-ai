# Chapter 5 audit notes

Reviewed: 29 September 2026

## Argument

The chapter proposes an operational context envelope: task state, facts, policy, history, provenance, and access. It applies relevance, authority, freshness, and permission tests; separates memory purposes; and gives a first-week source-mapping exercise.

## Editorial findings

- Reframed categorical assertions about where failures begin and what agents can do as possibilities and design risks.
- Defined the envelope as the book's proposed tool and required validation against actual cases.
- Separated Anthropic engineering guidance from independent impact research and scoped the NIST profile as voluntary risk-management guidance.
- Clarified that labeling content untrusted is not itself an access-control boundary. Prompt injection remains an evolving risk; enforce authority around consequential actions.
- Added instruction to follow company access and retention policies when sharing source material with an AI workspace.
- Example supplier, pricing, and refund situations are illustrative; no measured deployment claim is made.

## Open items

- Freshness, access, and retention obligations vary by data and jurisdiction; chapter gives design questions rather than legal advice.
- Final source-link, terminology, field-kit cross-reference, diagram, and multi-format checks remain open.

# Chapter 18 research notes

Evidence and law checked 29 September 2026. The chapter distinguishes operational accountability from legally allocated liability; legal duties are jurisdiction-, role-, and use-dependent.

## EU AI Act

- Current consolidated text is in force, with amendments from the AI Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, entered into force 27 July 2026). The Commission page states Annex III high-risk obligations apply from 2 December 2027 and Annex I product-embedded high-risk rules from 2 August 2028; transparency rules apply from August 2026.
- Article 26 assigns human oversight to natural persons with competence, training, authority, and support for high-risk deployments.
- Chapter text now accurately separates the duty from its application date and points readers to qualified legal review.
- This is not a complete compliance guide; other EU and national duties may apply.

## Accountability and recourse

- Definitions of execution, authority, responsibility, accountability, and liability are the book's working distinctions, not claims that all jurisdictions use identical terminology.
- The hiring-screening failure and its repair are explicitly illustrative, not real case history.
- Recourse provisions are operational recommendations. The chapter avoids stating a universal legally mandated appeal right for all automated decisions.
- NIST/Anthropic sources in earlier chapters support bounded governance observations, not a legal conclusion that accountability is non-delegable in every context.

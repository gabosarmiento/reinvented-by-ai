# Chapter 18 editorial review

Reviewed: 29 September 2026

## Findings

### High — accountability could be confused with legal liability

Definitions now explicitly operate as book terminology and distinguish operational ownership from legal liability.

### High — EU AI Act timetable and oversight obligations were time-sensitive

Updated with current consolidated text and Commission timeline: Annex III 2 December 2027, Annex I 2 August 2028, transparency provisions from August 2026. The Act requires competent, trained, authorized, and supported natural-person oversight for applicable high-risk deployment.

### Medium — recourse language could imply a universal legal appeal right

Framed recourse as an operational design recommendation and avoided universal legal claims.

The current pass also states explicitly in the chapter that its working terms are not jurisdiction-wide legal definitions and that rights to explanation, review, or appeal depend on applicable law and facts.

## Qualified legal review brief

The chapter uses accountability as a book-level operating concept and liability for legally allocated consequences. Its title is not a claim of a universal legal doctrine. Recourse is recommended as an operating practice unless applicable law creates a specific right. EU AI Act obligations depend on role, classification, and use. Ask qualified counsel to verify:

- EU AI Act application dates and provider/deployer terminology against the current consolidated text;
- whether the chapter's high-risk classification language covers the relevant use and exceptions accurately;
- human oversight wording, including competence, training, authority, and support;
- the division of provider and deployer responsibilities;
- whether the illustrative hiring-screening example implicates GDPR automated-decision rules, given the actual degree of human involvement;
- employment and labour law implications in the relevant jurisdictions;
- discrimination and equality law implications;
- recourse and appeal wording, distinguishing recommendations from enforceable rights;
- record retention and evidence obligations, including privacy limits;
- any wording a reader could interpret as legal advice.

Ask counsel to mark jurisdiction-specific caveats and any required changes before publication. This brief prepares the review; the qualified legal review remains an open release gate.
